## 1. Can a Texas PTA deliver telehealth? Supervision rules, rule citations, and the PT Compact

### Takeaway
Yes. Texas Board rule 22 TAC §322.5 (Telehealth) explicitly lets a PTA provide PT services via telehealth, under the same supervision standard as in person (§322.3(b)), with a supervising PT who holds a Texas license or a Texas compact privilege. Services must be synchronous audio-visual or audio. Texas has been in the PT Compact since 2017, and compact privileges (including PTA privileges) have been issued since January 2019. Under the Compact, practice happens where the patient is located. A Texas PTA can therefore buy privileges to treat patients in other compact states by telehealth, subject to each state's rules on PTAs.

### Cited Findings
- 22 TAC §322.5: PT telehealth services "must be provided by a physical therapist assistant under the supervision of a physical therapist who possesses a current unrestricted Texas license or Compact Privilege to practice in Texas." — [22 TAC §322.5 (Cornell LII)](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-5); [Justia TAC §322.5](https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-22/part-16/chapter-322/section-322-5)
- A PTA providing telehealth "is subject to the same standard of care that would apply to the provision of the same physical therapy service in an in-person setting", including the supervision of PTAs under PT Rules §322.3(b). — [22 TAC §322.5 (Cornell LII)](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-5); [TX Board FAQ #7: special rules for supervising a PTA via telehealth](https://ptot.texas.gov/7-is-there-any-special-rules-for-supervising-a-pta-who-is-providing-physical-therapy-services-via-telehealth/)
- Telehealth delivery "requires synchronous audiovisual or audio interaction between the physical therapist assistant and the patient/client, which may be accompanied by the use of asynchronous store and forward technology." This means asynchronous-only care (such as app messaging alone) does not by itself count as PTA telehealth treatment under §322.5. — [22 TAC §322.5 (Cornell LII)](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-5)
- General supervision (§322.3): the supervising PT "is responsible for and will participate in the patient's care", must be "on call and readily available when physical therapy services are being provided", and must hold documented conferences with the PTA about the patient. The PT sets how often these conferences happen. — [Justia TAC §322.3](https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-22/part-16/chapter-322/section-322-3); [Cornell §322.5 summary](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-5)
- The most current consolidated Board rules PDF is dated November 2025 ("PTrules_2025.112"). — [TX PT Rules PDF, 2025.11](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/PTrules_2025.112.pdf)
- The Board also publishes a FAQ on telehealth reimbursement and billing codes (FAQ #9). — [ptot.texas.gov FAQ list](https://ptot.texas.gov/category/faqs/pt-practice-faqs/page/2/)
- PT Compact: Texas joined via SB 317, signed June 9, 2017. Compact privileges for Texas licensees to practice elsewhere, and for others to practice in Texas, became available January 2, 2019. — [TX Board Compact page](https://ptot.texas.gov/compact/)
- Compact fees: Texas PTA compact privilege fee $50; Commission fee $45 (plus each destination state's own fee). Eligibility: a current license in a member home state, both states actively issuing privileges, and no encumbrance or discipline for 2 years. — [TX Board Compact page](https://ptot.texas.gov/compact/); [PT Compact map – TX](https://ptcompact.org/State/TX)
- The PT Compact covers both PTs and PTAs. — [CompHealth PT Compact guide](https://comphealth.com/resources/physical-therapy-mutliple-states-pt-compact)
- A compact privilege allows practice in a remote state "under the scope of practice of the state where the patient/client is located, whether the practice is in-person or via telehealth"; practice occurs where the patient is located. — [PT Compact Help Center: telehealth and privileges](https://help.ptcompact.org/hc/en-us/articles/42516132730523-Does-a-compact-privilege-allow-the-privilege-holder-to-practice-via-telehealth-in-a-remote-state)
- The PT Compact publishes a scope-of-practice resource (updated Aug 2025) comparing state rules. Each state's PTA supervision and telehealth rules differ. — [PT Compact Scope of Practice Resources 08-2025](https://www.ptcompact.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Scope-of-Practice-Resources-Updated-08-2025.pdf)
- Remote PTA job listings often require "an active PTA license in a compact state", which shows employers use the Compact to staff multistate telehealth PTAs. — [VirtualVocations listing: Compact Licensed Physical Therapy Assistant](https://www.virtualvocations.com/job/compact-licensed-physical-therapy-assistant-3096625-i.html)

### Inferences
- For telehealth patients located in Texas, a Texas-licensed PTA is fully permitted to deliver treatment by live video or phone, provided a Texas-licensed or privileged PT supervises and stays reachable. Asynchronous-only "check-ins" are better framed as RTM/monitoring support than as PTA treatment visits.
- The Compact is the main thing that makes a Texas PTA attractive to national virtual employers. Each extra state costs about $95 or more (Commission $45 plus that state's fee), and the PTA must follow that state's PTA supervision and telehealth rules. Some states are stricter about PTAs or telehealth.
- An employer typically pays for privileges, but this was not verified.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full text of §322.3(b) to confirm Texas-specific numeric supervision triggers (e.g., PT re-visit or reassessment frequency, PTA-to-PT ratios, home-health on-site requirements). Pull these from the Nov 2025 rules PDF.
- Effective date of the current §322.5 text not confirmed.
- Current count of PT Compact member and issuing states in 2026 not confirmed (ptcompact.org fetch blocked).
- Texas Medicaid/commercial payer rules on PTA-delivered telehealth were not researched. Medicare permission for PTA telehealth depends on extensions of CMS telehealth flexibilities, and its 2026 status was not verified.

## 2. Companies hiring PTAs for remote/virtual clinical roles (digital MSK, RTM, telehealth)

### Takeaway
The big digital MSK platforms (Hinge Health, Sword Health) hire mostly licensed PTs and DPTs for their remote clinician roles. Genuine remote PTA clinical postings do exist but are scarcer and lower-paid: one Plethy posting paid about $26–28/hr, and some compact-license "remote PTA" monitoring roles were found. Advertised "remote PTA" pay ranges are wide and noisy. One aggregator showed $20–$43/hr for "virtual PTA" listings.

## 3. Remote documentation and clinical-ops roles open to PTAs (audit, UR, MDS, billing, scheduling)

### Takeaway
Remote therapy audit, utilization review, and medical-review jobs exist in large numbers, about 541 "remote medical physical therapy audit" listings on Glassdoor in Oct 2026. Most are written for licensed PTs or OTs. PTAs are more competitive for therapy-adjacent ops roles: prior authorization, denials and appeals coordination, rehab scheduling, and some home health QA/coding positions. PT-level reviewer pay runs about $39–$64/hr. PTA-eligible ops roles were not individually priced.

### Cited Findings

Pay is about $43–$64/hr nationally and $39–$61/hr in Tennessee. — [ZipRecruiter WFH PT Auditor](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Work-From-Home-Physical-Therapist-Auditor); [ZipRecruiter WFH PT Auditor TN](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Work-From-Home-Physical-Therapist-Auditor/--in-Tennessee)

### Inferences
- PTAs most plausibly land remote ops roles at SNF and home-health rehab contract companies (Select Rehabilitation, Aegis, Genesis, Reliant, Encompass, Amedisys, and others), often by moving internally. - Coding certifications (e.g., HCS-D/COS-C for home health, CPC for billing) are the standard way for a PTA to qualify for remote reviewer or coder jobs. This is an inference; not verified against 2026 postings.

- No verified pay for PTA-level remote ops roles (scheduler, prior-auth, denials). These are likely priced as non-licensed admin roles (~$18–$25/hr), but this is unsourced.

## 4. Family-flexible in-person options near home (PRN home health, SNF, weekend, Luna) and Texas PRN rates

### Takeaway
PRN home health with per-visit pay is the most flexible in-person option for a Texas PTA with an infant. Texas postings show about $55–$60 per routine visit (ranging roughly $40–$65), often plus mileage. The Texas PTA mean wage is $34.64/hr ($72,060/yr, BLS May 2024), and Texas employs more PTAs than any other state. Luna advertises fully flexible per-visit in-home work but its Texas postings found were for PTs ($65–$77/visit); no PTA Luna postings were found.

### Cited Findings
- BLS OEWS May 2024: Texas had the highest PTA employment of any state (10,110), with a mean hourly wage of $34.64 and an annual mean of $72,060. ## 5. Texas PTA license renewal, CE, Jurisprudence exam, and keeping a license while not practicing

### Takeaway
Texas PTAs renew every two years by the end of their birth month. Renewal requires 20 continuing-competence hours, which include passing the Texas Jurisprudence Assessment Module (TX JAM, worth 2 CE hours, $48) and 1 hour of human-trafficking prevention training. The PTA renewal fee was reported as $184. A licensee who isn't practicing in Texas can choose inactive status, but it still requires CE, the JAM, and a renewal fee every two years, and is capped at 6 consecutive years.

### Cited Findings
- 20 CE hours per 2-year renewal for PTAs; renewal is due by the end of the birth month every two years. — [PT Progress: Texas renewal](https://www.ptprogress.com/?p=11832); [Advantis TX licensing guide (reviewed Sept 2026)](https://advantismed.com/travel-nurse-jobs/therapy/physical-therapy/texas/license)
- TX JAM passing score required; counts as 2 CE; costs $48 for renewing or restoring PT/PTA licensees. — [TX Board TX JAM page](https://ptot.texas.gov/pt-jam)
- At least 1 hour must be human trafficking prevention training, in addition to 2 hours of JAM. — [PT Progress](https://www.ptprogress.com/?p=11832); [Elite Learning TX PT/PTA 27-hr book 2025](https://ebooks.elitelearning.com/view/420355930/5/)
- The PTA renewal fee was $184 per the renewal form search extract. The forms date from 2023 and the fee may have changed. — [PT Renewal form 2023.09](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PT_Renewal_2023.09_fillable.pdf); [PT Renewal form 2023.10](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/PT_Renewal_2023.10_fillable.pdf)
- Late fees: expired 90 days or less, renewal fee plus 1/2 the renewal fee; expired more than 90 days, renewal fee plus a full renewal fee. — [PT Renewal form 2023.09](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PT_Renewal_2023.09_fillable.pdf)
- Inactive status (22 TAC §341.8) is the "voluntary termination of the right or privilege to practice" in Texas. It is available at renewal instead of renewing active, may be held "no more than six consecutive years," and is renewed every two years with a signed renewal application documenting board-approved CE, the inactive renewal fee, and a passing JAM score. — [22 TAC §341.8 (Cornell)](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-341-8); [Justia §341.8](https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-22/part-16/chapter-341/section-341-8)
- Board renewal forms and fee schedules are posted at ptot.texas.gov/forms. — [ptot.texas.gov/forms](https://ptot.texas.gov/forms)

### Inferences
- For a PTA doing remote ops or RTM work, the cheapest safe path is to keep the license active rather than inactive. Inactive status saves little, because CE and JAM are still required, and it bars clinical or telehealth practice. Many remote employers (RTM, chart review) also require an active, unencumbered license.
- A Compact privilege requires an active home-state license, so going inactive also forfeits privileges.

### Gaps
- The 2026 PTA active and inactive renewal fees were not confirmed. The $184 figure comes from 2023 forms, and the inactive fee amount was not found.
- Steps to return from inactive to active (e.g., extra CE) were not confirmed.
- Whether a remote non-clinical role (e.g., chart audit) itself requires an active Texas license depends on the employer and was not verified.
