# Remote Wellness, Coaching, Ergonomics & Self-Employment Paths for a Texas PTA + Kinesiology BS (as of Oct 2026)

Context: Texas resident, BS kinesiology + PTA license, baby due Jan 2027, wants flexible home-based work. Research done Oct 2026 via web search; several primary sites (nbhwc.org, ptot.texas.gov, USPh/iCIMS career pages) were blocked to direct fetch, so some facts come from search-result snippets of those official pages — flagged where relevant. ## 1. Baseline: What does a Texas PTA earn clinically (for comparison)?

### Takeaway
Texas PTA median is roughly $76K/yr (~$36.66/hr) per BLS May 2024 data, above the national median (~$65.5K). Nearly every remote non-clinical path below starts lower (typically $20–$35/hr employed), so the family should plan for a pay cut unless she keeps some per-diem clinical work.

### Cited Findings
- Texas PTA (SOC 31-2021) May 2024 BLS OEWS: median $76,250/yr, $36.66/hr; 11,230 jobs; 10th pct $30,180, 25th $60,990, 75th $82,710, 90th $95,230; national median $65,510 — [WageDex (repackages BLS OEWS)](https://wagedex.com/jobs/physical-therapist-assistants/texas) (secondary source; verify on bls.gov/oes; May 2025 OEWS data may now be released and would supersede)
- Remote "PTA"-tagged job listings on job boards show $69K–$100K, but these are mostly not true PTA clinical work (often home health, utilization review, or misc.) — [WeekdayDoc remote PTA listings](https://www.weekdaydoc.com/remote-pta-jobs?page=25) (low reliability aggregator)

### Inferences
- A PTA cannot deliver PT remotely without PT supervision; true "remote PTA" clinical jobs are rare. Keeping a part-time/PRN clinical shift (weekends when partner is home) likely remains the highest $/hour option (~$35–$45/hr PRN in TX is typical but not sourced here).
- Employed remote health coaching at $23–$30/hr full-time ≈ $48K–$62K/yr — roughly a $15K–$28K drop vs. TX PTA median.

## 2. Scope-of-practice: what can a Texas PTA do independently?

### Takeaway
In Texas, health/wellness/fitness programs are considered within the scope of PT practice, and the Texas PT board states there is no exception that lets licensees step outside PT rules when providing wellness interventions. A PTA may only provide PT services under PT supervision. Practical implication: she must NOT market herself as providing "physical therapy," "rehab," or treatment using her PTA credential independently; she can work as a fitness trainer/health coach/ergonomic assessor under those separate credentials, but she should present them as non-PT services and should avoid treating symptomatic/injured clients.

### Cited Findings
- Texas Board of PT Examiners FAQ (Health, Wellness, Fitness category) includes the question "Am I able to provide personal training using my physical therapy credentials?"; board position: programs that promote health, wellness and fitness are within the scope of physical therapy practice, and "there is no provision in the Act/Rules that provides an exception when providing health, wellness, and fitness interventions to a client" — [Texas PT/OT Board FAQ](https://ptot.texas.gov/category/faqs/pt-practice-faqs/health-wellness-fitness) (seen via search snippet; site blocked for direct fetch)
- PT Rule §322.1(a)(2)(B): a **PT** may, without referral, provide instructions to an asymptomatic person, including instruction to promote health, wellness, and fitness — [22 TAC §322.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-1)
- Role delineation: a PTA provides PT services only under the supervision of a PT, performing duties the PT assigns — [22 TAC §322.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-2)

### Inferences
- Because the board treats wellness as PT scope when done "using PT credentials," the safest model is: operate as an "NBHWC/ACE/NASM coach" or "CEAS ergonomic assessor," don't use "PTA" as the credential sold, don't use the words "physical therapy"/"therapeutic," keep clients asymptomatic, refer symptomatic people to a PT/physician, and carry fitness/coaching liability insurance. Mentioning the background ("former PTA, BS kinesiology") as biography is common, but she should ask the Board directly (they answer written questions) before marketing anything pelvic-health or rehab-flavored.
- Pelvic-floor/diastasis "rehab" for postpartum clients is a scope trap: pelvic floor therapy is PT; a fitness coach can do general postpartum exercise programming and refer to a pelvic-health PT.

## 3. Health & wellness coaching (NBHWC + remote employers)

### Takeaway
This is the most realistic employed, remote, W-2 path. NBHWC certification requires an approved training program + 50 logged sessions + associate's degree (her BS qualifies easily; degree can be in any field). Employers (Omada, Virta, etc.) typically require a bachelor's and NBHWC-approved training; pay is modest ($23–$30/hr typical at digital-health companies; $45–$60/hr for experienced contractor corporate-wellness coaches).

### Cited Findings
- NBHWC exam eligibility: complete an NBHWC Approved Training Program; complete 50 health & wellness coaching sessions meeting coaching-log requirements; associate's degree or higher (any field) OR 4,000 hours of work experience — [NBHWC Get Board Certified](https://nbhwc.org/get-board-certified/); [NBHWC eligibility](https://nbhwc.org/exam-eligibility-requirements/) (search snippets; site blocked to fetch)
- 2026 exam windows: Jul 20–Aug 8 (apply May 18–Jun 1) and Nov 30–Dec 14 (apply Sep 28–Oct 12, 2026) — [NBHWC](https://nbhwc.org/get-board-certified/)
- Omada health coach postings: bachelor's required (NBHWC cert may substitute in some); completion of an NBHWC-approved training program required; active NBHWC board certification a plus; DPP Lifestyle Coach cert and bilingual a bonus; remote-first, parental leave, benefits — [Omada via Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/omadahealth/jobs/7749392); [Huntr](https://huntr.co/job/5f9c00f59fad6900568aee03)
- Omada health coach listed at $23/hr, 35–45 hrs/week — [Wellfound Omada posting](https://wellfound.com/jobs/3157107-health-coach) (posting date unclear; may be older)
- Virta health coach ~$30/hr; Virta coach manager $69,600–$96,000 — [search summary of Virta postings](https://jobs.obvious.com/companies/virta-health/jobs/40185318-health-coach) (aggregator; verify)
- Corporate wellness part-time contractor health coach (Wellable): $50–$60/hr; another listing $45–50/hr; part-time 20 hrs/wk, 7am–7pm CST M–F; another remote role capped at 19.5 hrs/wk; requirement 3–5+ years coaching experience, board certification strongly preferred, private workspace — [NANP Career Center](https://jobs.nanp.org/job/health-coach-corporate-wellness/81282078); [WELCOA job archive](https://www.welcoa.org/?p=10237)
- Aggregate health-coach salary claims: ~$61.6K average (Salary.com/Glassdoor); entry-level $30K–$60K FT, part-time/contract $20–$50/hr — [Virtual Vocations blog](https://www.virtualvocations.com/blog/?p=601137) (secondary; the "BLS $63K median" claim in that source is questionable—BLS has no dedicated "health coach" occupation)

### Inferences

The 50 logged sessions can be done with practice clients during maternity leave (phone/video, flexible).

Corporate-wellness contractor roles ($45–60/hr) want 3–5 yrs coaching experience—so a 1–2 year ramp via entry-level role or private practice is likely.

## 4. Online personal training / exercise physiology / pre-postnatal coaching

### Takeaway
Kinesiology BS qualifies her directly for ACSM-EP (no other experience needed beyond CPR/AED). Entry certs cost ~$1K. Self-employed online coaching can pay well per client ($150–$400/mo) but income depends entirely on marketing and client retention; for a new parent this is realistically side income ($500–$2,000/mo) for the first 1–2 years. Pre/postnatal specialization (GGS CPPC) fits her life stage and is a credible niche.

### Cited Findings
- ACSM-EP eligibility: minimum bachelor's in exercise science, exercise physiology, or kinesiology + adult CPR/AED; exam $350 members / $460 non-members; retest $235; 140 items, 210 minutes; pass = 550 scaled — [Open Exam Prep summary](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-ep/introduction/credential-exam-facts); [GVSU certification page](https://www.gvsu.edu/exercisescience/certifications-123.htm) (ACSM.org itself not fetched; verify fees)
- NASM-CPT packages $999–$1,399 (frequent sales), ~4 months self-paced; ACE-CPT $989–$1,749 with frequent ~50% discounts, 3–6 months — [NASM 2026 cost comparison](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/certification/personal-trainer-certification-cost-comparison-2026-nasm-vs-issa-ace-acsm-cscs)
- Girls Gone Strong Pre- & Postnatal Coaching Certification (CPPC): typical ~$899–$1,500; presale discounts up to $600; enrollment opened Aug 23, 2026 (limited) — [GGS CPPC FAQ](https://go.girlsgonestrong.com/cppc-faq); [Born BIR 2026 guide](https://www.bornbir.com/blog/how-to-become-a-pre-and-postnatal-fitness-coach-in-2026)
- Online coach pricing: $150–$400/month per client for custom plans; ~10 clients part-time ≈ €1,500–€3,000/mo; 30 clients at $300/mo = $9,000/mo — [Coachway](https://coachway.io/articles/how-much-do-online-fitness-coaches-make/); [FitBudd](https://www.fitbudd.com/post/best-online-fitness-coach-pricing-models) (software vendors with incentive to promote; optimistic)
- Trainers offering online coaching average $52,518/yr vs $34,585 in-person-only (claimed survey) — [Exercise.com](https://www.exercise.com/grow/how-much-do-personal-trainers-make-training-online) (vendor data; low reliability)
- Employed remote online fitness coach role (Redefining Strength) advertised $72K–$100K/yr — [Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/education/online-fitness-coach-4929027) (single posting; likely requires experience)

### Inferences
- NSCA-CSCS also requires a bachelor's (she qualifies) but is athlete-focused; less useful for a home-based general/postnatal clientele.
- Platforms: Trainerize/TrueCoach are software she pays for (~$10–$100/mo) to run her own business; Future/Caliber/Ladder/Noom are employers/contractors with their own hiring — no verified 2026 pay found for them this session.
- Her personal postpartum experience + kinesiology degree + CPPC is a credible, marketable niche (postpartum return-to-exercise), as long as pelvic-floor treatment is referred to a pelvic-health PT.

### Gaps

- NSCA-CSCS 2026 fee not verified.

## 5. Remote ergonomics consulting (CEAS) and industrial injury prevention

### Takeaway
CEAS (Back School of Atlanta) explicitly lists PTAs as eligible; cost about $500 per level. Employers like Briotix Health (merged with Atlas Injury Prevention in 2024) and U.S. Physical Therapy's industrial division hire kinesiology/ergonomics staff and do virtual office/vehicle ergonomic assessments, but most "industrial sports medicine" jobs are onsite at warehouses/plants; fully-remote ergonomic roles are a minority. Pay ~$28–$35/hr for industrial injury-prevention techs.

### Cited Findings
- CEAS I target audience explicitly includes PTs, PTAs, OTs, OTAs, ATs, RNs, safety professionals, HR, office furniture sales, etc.; 2-day workshop (also hybrid/online) on OSHA-compliant ergonomics assessments for office, industrial, healthcare — [eMedEvents CEAS I hybrid listing](https://www.emedevents.com/online-cme-courses/live-webinar/ceas-i-ergonomics-assessment-certification-workshop-hybrid)
- CEAS available as 2-day course or online; ~$500 per level × 3 levels (~$1,500 total) — [PT Progress ergonomics certifications](https://www.ptprogress.com/ergonomics-certifications/) (article may be several years old; verify current price)
- Atlas Injury Prevention Solutions merged with Briotix Health in May 2024; serves industrial, distribution, transportation, construction, office ergonomics, healthcare — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/atlas-injury-prevention-solutions)
- Briotix/Atlas "Industrial Sports Medicine Professional" duties include functional employment testing, return-to-work, ergonomic assessments (onsite, virtual, office, vehicle); $28–$35/hr depending on credentials — [USPh/IISM postings via search](https://careers-usph.icims.com/jobs/33894/industrial-sports-medicine-professional/job) (snippet; page blocked)
- Industrial injury-prevention postings recruit "exercise science / exercise physiologist / kinesiologist / athletic trainer" — kinesiology degree is an accepted entry credential — [Recorder job board](https://jobs.recorder.com/job/352f3d50-193f-40db-961e-b227cb1002fe/exercise-science-exercise-physiologist-kinesiologist-athletic-trainer-industrial-injury-preve)
- Virtual ergonomic assessments are done via employee video submission/live video; Work-Fit markets them — [Work-Fit blog](https://www.work-fit.com/blog/why-choose-a-virtual-ergonomic-assessment/); remote ergonomist roles include asynchronous video-based evaluations — [Remotive certified ergonomist posting](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/all-others/certified-ergonomist-4376765)

### Inferences
- Freelance virtual office ergonomic assessments (sold to small businesses/HR, or via subcontracting to ergonomics firms) are feasible from home and asynchronous (video review) — good for an infant schedule — but sales are hard; realistically side income initially.
- Fully remote employed ergonomist roles often prefer CPE/CAE or a master's; CEAS + kinesiology + PTA is competitive for entry-level/hybrid roles. Industrial early-intervention roles are mostly onsite in Texas distribution hubs (DFW, Houston, Austin) — hybrid at best.

## 7. Remote teaching / tutoring

### Takeaway
College adjunct teaching of kinesiology/exercise science generally requires a master's (or 18 graduate hours in the discipline) under SACSCOC (Texas's accreditor) — she does not qualify with a BS. PTA-program adjunct roles sometimes accept PTAs for lab/clinical instruction (onsite). NPTE-PTA/anatomy tutoring is a plausible side income.

### Cited Findings
- SACSCOC guideline: faculty teaching baccalaureate or general-education courses should hold a master's with ≥18 graduate semester hours in the teaching discipline; graduate teaching needs a doctorate/terminal degree; 18-hour standard is a guideline, institutions may justify alternative qualifications — [SACSCOC Faculty Credentials](https://www.sacscoc.org/app/uploads/2019/07/faculty-credentials.pdf)

### Inferences
- Technical/associate programs (PTA programs) can justify credentialing on professional licensure/experience, so a licensed PTA with experience may teach PTA-program lab courses (usually in person) — not verified for specific Texas colleges.
- Tutoring platforms (Wyzant, Varsity Tutors, Chegg-type) pay roughly $15–$40/hr — not verified this session.

### Gaps

## 9. Scams / MLM "coach" traps to avoid

### Takeaway
"Health coach" is an unregulated title heavily used by MLMs (Optavia, Arbonne, etc.) that pay mainly through recruitment and product sales; avoid any "coach" role requiring buying product, starter kits, or recruiting.

### Cited Findings
- FTC sent Arbonne an April 2020 warning letter over COVID treatment claims and deceptive earnings claims, plus a Notice of Penalty Offenses on health claims substantiation — [FTC warning letter](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/warning-letters/covid-19-letter_to_arbonne_international_llc.pdf); [Truth in Advertising](https://www.truthinadvertising.org/pyramid-allegations-filed-dsa-top-seller-arbonne/)
- Optavia "coaches" are typically non-credentialed customers in an MLM structure earning from recruitment — [GetCurex Optavia review](https://getcurex.com/glp1-blog/optavia-diet-guide-rules-benefits-foods-to-eat) (secondary); dietitian critique of MLM nutrition coaches — [Abby Langer Nutrition](https://abbylangernutrition.com/another-cog-in-the-wheel-the-issue-with-nutrition-mlm-coaches/)

### Inferences
- Red flags: upfront fees for "certification" bundled with a business-in-a-box, income claims, recruiting downline, "work from home as a coach" ads with no employer W-2. Legit employers (Omada, Virta, Quantum Health) pay hourly/salary and never charge the employee.
- Also be cautious of non-NBHWC "health coach certifications" marketed as career guarantees (e.g., heavy-marketing schools); NBHWC-approved status is the credential employers ask for.

### Gaps
- None critical.

## 10. Summary ranking for a new parent (inference, not sourced)
- Most viable steady remote income: employed health coach (NBHWC-track) — ~$23–$30/hr FT, benefits; part-time contractor roles $45–60/hr later with experience.
- Best scheduling flexibility: asynchronous online coaching/programs, virtual ergonomic video reviews, content — but side income at first.
- Highest $/hr: keep PRN clinical PTA shifts (~TX median $36.66/hr) alongside remote work.
- Not viable without more school: college adjunct (master's needed).
- Startup costs: ACSM-EP ~$460; NASM/ACE ~$500–$1,400; CPPC ~$900–$1,500; CEAS ~$500/level; NBHWC-approved program likely several $K (unverified).
