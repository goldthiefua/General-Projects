# Keep the license, build a remote bridge

A Texas PTA who also has a kinesiology bachelor's has real remote options, but the honest picture is narrower than most "non-clinical PT" lists suggest. **The best-fit paths for her are**: clinical implementation and support jobs at rehab and home-health software companies; workers' compensation claims adjusting (a cheap Texas license plus her bachelor's); telehealth and remote-monitoring PTA work, which Texas law allows but which is scarce and lower-paid; and entry-level prior-authorization or utilization-management intake jobs, which are easy to get but pay like clerical work. Health coaching, ergonomics and online postpartum fitness are the most flexible around a baby, but they usually start as side income. **The most widely advertised "clinical reviewer" jobs are not open to her.** eviCore, Evolent, Optum and BCBS therapy reviewer roles require a full physical therapist (PT) license, and Sedgwick and CorVel case managers usually need a nursing license. Pay is the hard part. A Texas PTA earns roughly **$72,000–$76,000 a year (about $35–$37 an hour)**, and most remote entry jobs start between $16 and $30 an hour. The plan that holds up best is to **keep her license active and keep one or two flexible in-person PRN days** while she moves into a remote role during 2027. With about 13 working weeks left before a January 2027 due date, the smart move this fall is to pick up cheap, quick credentials and line up flexible work for after leave. Starting a brand-new full-time job right before delivery is the riskier choice.

*A note on sources: the researchers worked on October 6, 2026. Many official sites (the Texas PT board, CCMC, NBHWC, some job boards) blocked direct page reads, so a number of facts come from search-engine extracts of those pages. Salary figures from aggregators such as ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor are estimates. Check any fee or pay figure on the linked page before making a decision.*

## Her clinical paycheck sets a $35-an-hour bar most remote jobs miss

The benchmark is what she earns now. Texas employs more PTAs than any other state, and federal wage data for May 2024 put the Texas PTA **mean at $34.64 an hour ($72,060 a year)** ([BLS OES 31-2021](https://www.bls.gov/Oes/current/oes312021.htm)) and the **median at about $76,250 ($36.66 an hour)**. The middle half of Texas PTAs earn between about $61,000 and $83,000 ([WageDex, repackaging BLS](https://wagedex.com/jobs/physical-therapist-assistants/texas)). The table below compares every path to that figure. "Fit" combines three things: whether her credentials qualify today, whether the pay comes close to what she earns now, and how well the schedule works with an infant at home.

The table shows the main tension. **The paths that pay close to her current wage (software, adjusting, recruiting) are full-time, business-hours jobs.** The paths that bend best around a baby (coaching, ergonomics, PRN visits) either pay per piece of work or take a year or two to build. "Remote" does not mean "no childcare." Assistant-level remote utilization review is described as full-time, 9-to-5, 100% remote work ([Alternative Healthcare Careers](https://www.alternativehealthcarecareers.com/blog/remote-jobs-for-pt-and-ot-assistants)). Digital coaching employers book members into scheduled call blocks. The family should plan on at least part-time childcare or a split-shift arrangement for any full-time remote job.

## Remote PTA treatment is legal in Texas but scarce and lower-paid

Texas explicitly allows PTAs to treat patients by telehealth. Board rule 22 TAC §322.5 says telehealth physical therapy "must be provided by a physical therapist assistant under the supervision of a physical therapist" who holds a Texas license or compact privilege. Care must be **live video or audio**, held to the same standard of care as an in-person visit ([22 TAC §322.5](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-5)). The supervising PT must stay on call and readily available, and must hold documented conferences with the PTA about each patient ([22 TAC §322.3](https://regulations.justia.com/states/texas/title-22/part-16/chapter-322/section-322-3)). Messaging a patient through an app does not count as a PTA treatment visit by itself.

Texas has been in the **PT Compact** since 2017, and it covers PTAs as well as PTs. A Texas PTA can buy a "privilege" to treat patients in other member states by telehealth. The Texas PTA privilege fee is **$50**, the Commission fee is **$45**, and some destination states add their own fee ([Texas Board Compact page](https://ptot.texas.gov/compact/)). Care counts as happening where the patient is, so each state's PTA rules apply ([PT Compact Help Center](https://help.ptcompact.org/hc/en-us/articles/42516132730523-Does-a-compact-privilege-allow-the-privilege-holder-to-practice-via-telehealth-in-a-remote-state)). This is worth knowing because remote PTA postings often require "an active PTA license in a compact state" ([VirtualVocations](https://www.virtualvocations.com/job/compact-licensed-physical-therapy-assistant-3096625-i.html)). She does not need to buy privileges in advance. She can wait until a job requires them.

The catch is supply. The big digital physical therapy companies hire PTs, not PTAs, for clinical work. **Hinge Health requires a DPT** for its remote clinician roles ([The Non-Clinical PT](https://thenonclinicalpt.com/hinge-health/)), and Sword Health's flexible remote postings are titled "Physical Therapist" ([Sword posting](https://jobs.inwomenshealth.com/companies/sword-health/jobs/48058596-physical-therapist-us-remote-only-variable-hour-full-time)). Researchers found no PTA posting at Luna, the in-home outpatient company known for letting clinicians pick their own visit counts. Its Texas postings were for PTs at $65–$77 a visit ([Luna on Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/lunaphysicaltherapy/ed9cf8c6-4b35-4b5a-ab59-4680ff276f74)). Real remote PTA roles do exist, but they are smaller and pay less. **Plethy posted a remote, flexible-schedule PTA role at $26–$28 an hour**, Vori Health turned up in remote-PTA searches ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-pta-l-remote-jobs.html)), and "virtual PTA" listings cluster at **$20–$43 an hour** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Virtual-Pta)). These jobs mostly involve remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM): checking in with patients by video, making sure they follow the PT's plan, reporting changes and documenting. A promising and under-used angle is the *clinics* that buy RTM software such as Limber, which Net Health now owns ([Net Health](https://www.nethealth.com/blog/net-health-advances-rehab-therapy-care-with-acquisition-of-keet-health-from-webpt/)). A local outpatient group running an RTM program is more likely to hire a PTA for remote follow-ups than the software vendor is.

The strongest pay-to-flexibility option is not remote at all. **PRN home health pays Texas PTAs about $55–$60 per routine visit, often plus mileage.** Examples include BrightStar Care San Antonio at $60 per visit plus mileage and travel time, and Dallas-area postings at $55–$60 ([Glassdoor Texas](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/texas-home-health-pta-pay-per-visit-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,5_IS1347_KO6,35.htm)). That works out to roughly $40–$50 an hour of treatment time, before unpaid driving and documentation. She chooses which days to take, so she could work Saturdays when her husband is home or a few hours in a nap window. A day or two of this also keeps her clinical skills current and gives her license real value. Many remote employers, from RTM clinics to chart-review shops, ask for an active, unrestricted license.

Keeping the license costs little. Texas PTAs renew every two years by the end of their birth month. Renewal takes **20 continuing-education hours**, including the Texas Jurisprudence module (worth 2 hours, $48) and 1 hour of human-trafficking training ([PT Progress](https://www.ptprogress.com/?p=11832); [TX JAM](https://ptot.texas.gov/pt-jam)). The renewal fee was $184 on 2023 forms ([renewal form](https://ptot.texas.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PT_Renewal_2023.09_fillable.pdf)). **Going "inactive" saves almost nothing.** It still requires CE, the jurisprudence module and a fee, it bars all clinical and telehealth work, it cancels any compact privileges, and it can last no more than six consecutive years ([22 TAC §341.8](https://law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-341-8)).

## Payer-side jobs split sharply: adjuster and prior-auth yes, PT clinical reviewer no

This section covers the jobs she has probably seen most online, and it is where she needs the most candor. **"PT clinical reviewer" jobs at insurance companies and benefit managers are not realistic for a PTA.** eviCore (Cigna) has required a PT license plus **ten years of clinical experience** for its remote Physical Therapist clinical reviewer ([Career.com, 2023 posting](https://www.career.com/job/cigna/clinical-reviewer-physical-therapist-evicore-remote/j202305301111122315345)). Evolent requires "current, unrestricted state licensure as a Physical Therapist" ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/job-listing/clinical-reviewer-physical-therapy-evolent-JV_KO0,34_KE35,42.htm?jl=1009835328393)). Optum's PT clinical review roles pay $87K–$106K and are PT jobs ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Optum-Clinical-Review-Physical-Therapy)). Gainwell's medical reviewer pays $85K–$100K and requires a PT ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-clinical-review-physical-therapist-remote-jobs.html)). One remote utilization review posting accepts RN, DC, PT, DPT, LVN, OT and acupuncturist licenses, and leaves **PTA off the list** ([RemoteNursingJobs](https://remotenursingjobs.com/job/utilization-review-health-care-professional-pt-lvn-lpn-remote/)). The likely reason is structural. Deciding whether care is medically necessary is a judgment insurers give to clinicians who can practice independently, and a PTA's license is supervised by design. The roughly 700 Indeed hits for "utilization review PTA" are keyword matches, not 700 jobs she qualifies for ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-utilization-review-pta-jobs-jobs.html)).

There are narrow exceptions worth watching. A rehab career site reports that "some companies make exceptions for assistants" and names **Anthem (Elevance), myNEXUS (home-health utilization management, now part of Elevance) and Numotion (wheelchair seating and mobility authorization)** as having hired PTAs and COTAs as utilization reviewers. These roles were full-time and business hours, and typically required several years of clinical experience ([Alternative Healthcare Careers](https://www.alternativehealthcarecareers.com/blog/remote-jobs-for-pt-and-ot-assistants)). Researchers could not confirm a 2026 posting that literally lists PTA. She should set job alerts for these employers and read each posting's license list before applying.

The open door is **non-licensed prior-authorization and utilization-management intake.** These jobs check insurance benefits, enter authorization requests and handle phone calls. Elevance's "Utilization Management Representative I" is an example: it is virtual except for in-person training ([Lensa](https://lensa.com/job-v1/elevance-health/remote/utilization-management-nurse/00150c0eaea0d500d7d0e5fbaa8c4b43)). Remote prior-auth jobs in Texas average **$19.47 an hour**, mostly $16–$21 ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Remote-Prior-Authorization/--in-Texas)). Specialty pharmacy roles such as Onco360 in Houston ($23) and Soleo Health ($23–$27) pay more ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/texas-remote-medical-prior-authorization-specialist-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,5_IS1347_KO6,51.htm)). That is a **$10–$18-an-hour pay cut** from clinical work. These jobs make sense only as a bridge into experience that leads somewhere better, or if being home matters more than income for a season.

**Workers' compensation claims adjusting is the strongest payer-side path that doesn't need a nursing license.** Texas requires an all-lines adjuster license, which covers workers' comp claims. She can earn it by passing a Pearson VUE exam (150 questions, 70% to pass) or by completing a TDI-approved 40-hour pre-licensing course, which waives the exam. After that she applies through Sircon for **$50** and gets fingerprinted ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/agent/adjuster-all-lines-apply.html); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/claims-adjuster/texas-statutes-rules/texas-adjuster-licensing)). Texas Mutual in Austin pays WC claims adjusters an average of **about $68,000**, with claims adjusters ranging $60K–$81K ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/Texas-Mutual-Workers-Comp-Claims-Adjuster-Salaries-E19924_D_KO13,41.htm)). Remote Texas adjusters average about $60,000 ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Remote-Claims-Adjuster/--in-Texas)). Her PTA background is a real edge in interviews. She knows how to read therapy notes, judge whether the visit count makes sense, and understand functional capacity evaluations and return-to-work plans. The license course is online and self-paced, so she can finish it before the baby arrives. The unknown is whether a given employer will hire her fully remote or hybrid near Austin.

Workers' comp *case manager* jobs are a step beyond. **Sedgwick field case managers need an RN, or a graduate degree plus CCM, CDMS or CRC** ([Sedgwick posting](https://workopia.io/jobs/4905afd60c49d0db789c005e113eb849)), and a CorVel posting required an RN ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-corvel-case-manager-jobs.html)). Genex asks for a "current, unrestricted license or certification" in a health discipline ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Genex-Case-Management)). A PTA license might pass that test, but she should ask the recruiter directly.

The certifications are where people waste money, so here is the plain version. **ACM is RN or social worker only** ([ACMA](https://acmaweb.org/acm/default.aspx?nav=faq)). **CCM is uncertain to unlikely.** Its license route requires a license that allows *independent* practice, which a PTA license does not ([Practice Test Geeks](https://practicetestgeeks.com/ccm/ccm-certification-requirements)). Its degree route requires a bachelor's that included supervised case-management field experience, plus 24 months of case management work ([CCMC](https://yourcommission.org/blog/know-you-apply-are-you-eligible-take-ccm-exam)). **CDMS is realistic.** It accepts a bachelor's in any field plus **2,080 paid hours (one full-time year)** in disability, case management, workers' comp or wage-replacement work ([CDMS](https://cdms.org/get-certified/cdms-certification/eligibility-criteria-and-requirements)). After a year as a WC adjuster she would qualify, and CDMS is one of the credentials Sedgwick lists. The old utilization review certificates CPUR and CPHM have been discontinued ([allnurses](https://allnurses.com/certified-professional-healthcare-management-cphm-t543335/)), so she should not pay anyone for them.

## Software, billing and recruiting reward her clinic know-how

**Rehab and home-health software companies are where her clinical experience earns the most remotely.** They hire clinicians to set up the software at clinics and agencies, train staff, and support customers. Raintree Systems, which sells therapy EHR and billing software, is described as remote-first. It has posted remote "Clinical Implementation Consultant" roles, and Glassdoor shows implementation consultants earning **about $72K–$109K** ([Raintree careers](https://www.raintreeinc.com/careers/); [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/Raintree-California-Salaries-EI_IE27251.0,8_IL.9,19_IS2280.htm)). **Homecare Homebase (HCHB), headquartered in Dallas,** hires clinical implementation consultants for home health and hospice agencies. That role requires a bachelor's, **3+ years in home health or hospice**, and software training experience, and about 46% of HCHB jobs are remote ([BuiltIn Austin](https://www.builtinaustin.com/company/homecare-homebase/jobs); [Hired](https://www.hired.com/job/sr-implementation-consultant-hearst-merged-accounts)). WellSky clinical consultants report $82K–$100K at one to three years, from a small sample ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salary/WellSky-Clinical-Consultant-Salaries-E4942_D_KO8,27.htm)). MedBridge, which makes home exercise and education software, hires fully remote, lists Texas as a hiring state, and has clinical content roles ([MedBridge posting](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/Recruiting/Jobs/Details/2779226)). WebPT, the biggest outpatient therapy EMR, has not been absorbed by a larger company. In January 2026 it sold its Keet outcomes product to Net Health ([Net Health](https://www.nethealth.com/blog/net-health-advances-rehab-therapy-care-with-acquisition-of-keet-health-from-webpt/)), and Net Health is also worth targeting. The setting where she has worked decides which vendor to target. Home health or nursing-home experience points to HCHB, WellSky and Net Health. Outpatient experience points to WebPT, Raintree and MedBridge. If she has been her clinic's go-to person for the EMR, that belongs at the top of her resume. Entry usually starts in customer support or onboarding and moves up from there.

**Epic analyst jobs at hospitals can't be entered from the outside.** Individuals cannot buy Epic certification; an employer must sponsor it ([HealthcareITLeaders](https://www.healthcareitleaders.com/blog/how-to-become-epic-certified/)). Postings commonly let *internal* hires certify within about 120 days but require *external* hires to be certified already ([PIH Health](https://jobs.pihhealth.org/job/whittier/epic-application-analyst-i-ii-iii-rehab-and-ltc/912/81999927696)). UT Southwestern in Dallas has posted a Senior Epic Rehab Analyst ([UTSW](https://jobs.utsouthwestern.edu/job/23369559/senior-epic-rehab-analyst/)). This is a multi-year path. She would need to work PRN at a Texas hospital system that uses Epic, volunteer as a super-user or trainer during upgrades, and then transfer internally.

**Coding and billing are cheap to start and hard to break into.** The CPC exam costs about **$399–$499** ([Practice Test Geeks](https://practicetestgeeks.com/cpc/certification)). Newly certified coders in 2025–26 report that most postings want one to two years of experience, and some have applied to 50 or more jobs ([AAPC forum](https://www.aapc.com/discuss/threads/cpc-a-looking-for-entry-level-positions.205869/)). Remote PT billing jobs pay about **$18–$20 an hour** ([Bodies in Balance PT](https://bodies-in-balance-physical-therapy.careerplug.com/jobs/2114742)). Her real advantage is knowing therapy billing codes and documentation rules (the 97xxx codes, the 8-minute rule, plan-of-care certification), which points toward therapy compliance and denials roles rather than basic data entry. Clinical documentation integrity (CDI) is effectively closed to her. The CCDS credential requires three years of inpatient CDI work, and nurses and coders dominate hiring ([ACDIS](https://www.acdis.org/articles/acdis-update-requirements-and-experience-necessary-test-ccds-credential)).

**Therapy staffing recruiting is one of the easiest fully remote pivots to get into.** Aya Healthcare's remote Travel Therapy Recruiter has advertised **$60,000 base plus commission**, requiring a bachelor's (her kinesiology degree counts) and at least a year of metrics-driven sales or staffing experience ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/human-resources/travel-therapy-recruiter-5283200)). Former clinicians have credibility with the PTs and OTs they recruit, but the job is quota-driven. Health-plan care navigation at companies like Quantum Health or Included Health is genuinely remote but pays about **$40K–$52K** on scheduled shifts ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/t/Most-Popular-Types-Of-Remote-Health-Insurance-Navigator-Jobs)).

## Coaching and ergonomics flex around a baby but start small

There is a legal boundary she must respect first. The Texas PT board treats health, wellness and fitness programs as part of physical therapy practice. It says there is "no provision" exempting licensees who provide wellness services using their PT credentials ([Texas Board FAQ](https://ptot.texas.gov/category/faqs/pt-practice-faqs/health-wellness-fitness)), and a PTA may only provide PT services under a PT's supervision ([22 TAC §322.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/22-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-322-2)). In practice, any independent coaching or fitness business should be sold under her **coaching or fitness certifications, not "PTA" or "physical therapy."** She should work with healthy clients who have no symptoms and refer anyone with pain or injury to a PT. Postpartum pelvic-floor *treatment* is PT work. General postpartum exercise coaching is not. Before marketing anything that sounds like rehab, she should email the Board for a written answer.

**Health and wellness coaching is the most realistic employed remote option in this group.** NBHWC board certification requires three things: an approved training program, 50 logged coaching sessions, and at least an associate's degree in any field, which she already has ([NBHWC](https://nbhwc.org/exam-eligibility-requirements/)). Omada requires a bachelor's and completion of an NBHWC-approved program for its remote coaches ([Omada on Greenhouse](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/omadahealth/jobs/7749392)). Pay starts modestly: one Omada listing showed **$23 an hour** ([Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/jobs/3157107-health-coach)), and Virta coaches earn about $30 ([Virta via aggregator](https://jobs.obvious.com/companies/virta-health/jobs/40185318-health-coach)). The upside comes later. Part-time corporate-wellness contractor coaches earn **$45–$60 an hour for about 20 hours a week**, but those roles want three to five years of coaching experience ([NANP Career Center](https://jobs.nanp.org/job/health-coach-corporate-wellness/81282078)). The 2026 application window for the December exam runs September 28 to October 12, 2026 ([NBHWC](https://nbhwc.org/get-board-certified/)), which is too soon for her. The realistic target is a 2027 exam, with practice coaching sessions logged by phone during maternity leave. She should be wary of "health coach" offers from MLMs like Optavia or Arbonne, which earn mainly through recruiting and product sales. The FTC has warned Arbonne over deceptive earnings claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/warning-letters/covid-19-letter_to_arbonne_international_llc.pdf)). Any "job" that asks her to buy a starter kit is not a job.

**Her kinesiology degree opens fitness certifications directly.** The ACSM Exercise Physiologist (ACSM-EP) requires a kinesiology or exercise science bachelor's plus CPR/AED, and the exam costs **$350–$460** ([Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-ep/introduction/credential-exam-facts)). The Girls Gone Strong Pre- and Postnatal Coaching Certification costs about $899–$1,500 ([GGS](https://go.girlsgonestrong.com/cppc-faq)) and fits a postpartum niche she will soon know firsthand. Online coaching can bring in **$150–$400 a month per client** ([Coachway](https://coachway.io/articles/how-much-do-online-fitness-coaches-make/)), but those figures come from software vendors with an interest in optimism, and income depends entirely on finding clients. For a new parent, this is realistic side income for the first year or two, not a replacement salary.

**Ergonomics is a good credential with mostly onsite jobs.** The CEAS ergonomics certification explicitly lists PTAs as eligible and is offered online or hybrid ([eMedEvents](https://www.emedevents.com/online-cme-courses/live-webinar/ceas-i-ergonomics-assessment-certification-workshop-hybrid)), at about **$500 per level** ([PT Progress](https://www.ptprogress.com/ergonomics-certifications/)). Employers such as Briotix Health (which merged with Atlas Injury Prevention in 2024) and U.S. Physical Therapy's industrial division hire kinesiology graduates for injury prevention at **$28–$35 an hour**, but most of that work is at warehouses and plants ([USPh posting](https://careers-usph.icims.com/jobs/33894/industrial-sports-medicine-professional/job); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/atlas-injury-prevention-solutions)). Virtual office ergonomic assessments based on video review exist ([Work-Fit](https://www.work-fit.com/blog/why-choose-a-virtual-ergonomic-assessment/)). They suit a nap schedule well, but the work has to be sold or subcontracted, so expect side income at first.

On teaching: college kinesiology courses in Texas generally require a master's with 18 graduate hours in the subject ([SACSCOC](https://www.sacscoc.org/app/uploads/2019/07/faculty-credentials.pdf)). PTA-program adjunct roles, however, have accepted licensed PTAs with a bachelor's and about four years of experience ([Stanbridge](https://apply.workable.com/stanbridge/jobs/view/E886D63781.md)). Those roles are mostly in-person lab teaching, so they are a later, part-time option rather than a work-from-home one.

## A month-by-month plan around a January 2027 due date

The plan follows one principle: **don't give up a paycheck and leave benefits for an uncertain remote job right before delivery.** Use this fall for cheap credentials and groundwork, use leave for low-effort learning, and start the serious job search in spring 2027 while a PRN clinical day covers the income gap. Before any move, she should check how her current employer's maternity leave would work and compare it with what a new employer would offer a brand-new hire.

## Conclusion

Her two credentials do different jobs. The **PTA license** is what makes her valuable for clinical work, software vendors and remote monitoring, so letting it lapse would close more doors than it saves money. The **kinesiology bachelor's** quietly qualifies her for the adjuster, recruiter, ACSM-EP and CDMS routes that a PTA with only an associate's degree couldn't reach, and it is easy to undersell on a resume. The "clinical reviewer" jobs that dominate online advice are mostly closed to her. That is not a weakness in her background. It reflects how insurers split medical-necessity decisions from supervised care. Knowing it saves her from spending months applying to jobs she cannot get.

The largest unknowns are how many PTA-eligible remote jobs exist in any given month (postings are sporadic and the researchers could not confirm 2026 listings that name PTAs), and whether Texas employers will hire adjusters and implementation staff fully remote rather than hybrid. A two-track plan handles both risks: a flexible PRN clinical day protects income, and one cheap credential earned before January points toward the remote role. The family can then judge 2027 offers on schedule fit and pay rather than on urgency.
